The problem
Why this exists

A token nobody will buy
is not a liquid asset

The industry solved issuance and declared victory. But an investor who cannot get out of a position in a solar plant or a fleet of machines has not bought a liquid instrument — they have bought a private placement with a nicer interface.

🚫

No secondary market

A holder who needs cash before maturity has one option: find a private buyer themselves, negotiate blind, and hope. Most simply hold something they no longer want.

🎲

Prices nobody stands behind

Where a price exists it is usually the issuer's own valuation. Nobody has committed capital at that number, so it tells you what the seller hopes, not what the asset is worth.

💸

AMMs are the wrong shape

A constant-product curve prices a token against inventory. It has no view on whether the machine still runs, whether the operator still reports, or whether the cashflow arrived.

💡
The exit is what makes the entry investable
Retail capital buys an RWA for its yield — but only if it believes it can get out. Build a credible exit and you have not just added a feature; you have made the primary market work. That is the whole thesis of DobDex.

Chapter 00

Where DobDex sits

DobDex is not a standalone exchange. It is the last stage of a pipeline that already runs on Stellar mainnet: an asset gets validated, tokenized, and starts distributing yield. DobDex is what lets the holder of that position leave before maturity.

Stage 01 · Live

DobValidator

Validation agents review the operator's documents, contracts and reported cashflow, and produce a TRUFA score from 0 to 100.

▶
Stage 02 · Live

Token Studio

The validated asset is tokenized into a pool with participation shares. This is where an investor buys in.

▶
Stage 03 · Live

Distribution

Revenue from the asset is paid out to shareholders in USDC through the distribution contracts, on a schedule.

▶
Stage 04 · In build

DobDex

The holder opens an exit. Liquidity Nodes price it, the settlement router pays USDC, and an anchor turns that into local currency.

What DobDex is

A secondary exit venue. Holders sell positions they already own, for USDC, to Liquidity Nodes who choose to buy them. The protocol's job is to make that trade fair, bounded and observable — not to be the counterparty.

What DobDex is not

Not a redemption window, not a pegged swap, and not a promise of instant liquidity. The oracle prices the asset; the exit price is whatever a Liquidity Node will actually pay for it today.

Chapter 01

How an asset gets priced

Assets do not price themselves. The TRUFA score is produced off-chain by DobValidator, published on-chain with a timestamp, and consumed by the exit contracts under a hard freshness rule.

1
Assessment

DobValidator scores the asset

Validation agents review the operator's documentation, contracts and reported cash flows and produce a TRUFA score from 0 to 100. The score answers one question: how much should a stranger trust what this asset says about itself?

trufa_score: u32 // 0 .. 100
2
Publication

The score is written on-chain with a timestamp

Only the validator authority can publish, and every publication carries the ledger timestamp. Anyone can read the current score and its age — the input to every exit price is public before any exit is opened.

score_reader.publish_score(asset, score, ledger_ts)
3
Classification

The classifier turns the score into a tier

The tier determines the spread band Liquidity Nodes may bid inside, whether the hot reserve can be touched, and whether the asset is restricted to LP-only routing. A score change that crosses a boundary re-prices every future exit on that asset immediately.

(tier, min_bps, max_bps, policy) = classify(score)
4
Freshness gate

A stale score blocks the exit — it does not guess

If the last publication is older than STALENESS_SECS, score_reader reverts and no exit can be opened on that asset until the validator refreshes it. Refusing to quote is a safer failure than quoting on data nobody stands behind.

STALENESS_SECS = 86_400 // 24h, multisig-configurable
⚠
The score prices the asset. It does not price the exit.
A TRUFA score tells you how much confidence the asset's reported performance deserves. It does not tell you what someone will pay you for your position today — that is a liquidity question, answered by Liquidity Nodes bidding real USDC. DobDex keeps these two things separate on purpose, and does not offer a pegged or guaranteed redemption at score value.
Chapter 02

Five tiers, five different exits

The tier is the contract between the protocol and its users: it fixes in advance what a given score band is allowed to cost and how fast it can settle. Select a tier to see how it routes.

A band, not a price

The tier fixes the range a bid may fall in. Where inside the range it lands depends on how much capital is competing for that exit, how large the position is, and how deep the vault is for that tier. Two identical scores can clear at different prices on different days — that is the market working, not a bug.

Downgrades are not retroactive

A tier change applies to exits opened after it. An exit already inside its auction window keeps the band it opened with, so a seller cannot have the terms moved underneath them mid-auction. Settled exits are final.

Chapter 03

Anatomy of an exit

Every exit takes one of three paths, decided by the tier and the size of the position. Buying into an asset in the first place happens on Token Studio; DobDex only handles the way out.

Path 1

Instant from reserve

High score, small ticket, healthy reserve. The vault fills at the tier's floor spread and the seller has USDC in under a minute. Available only while the reserve stays above hot_reserve_floor.

Path 2

Auctioned to Liquidity Nodes

The default for anything larger or lower-scored. Nodes bid inside the tier band for 30 minutes; the best bid wins. The seller sees the clearing discount before signing the settlement.

Path 3

Queued, publicly

No acceptable bid arrived. The exit takes a numbered FIFO position visible on-chain and is served when capital enters that tier. The seller can walk away from the queue at any time.

Step by step
1
Open

The holder opens an exit from their wallet

The seller signs with Freighter (or any Soroban-compatible wallet) to authorize the position for exit. exit_auction.open_exit immediately calls score_reader: a stale or missing score stops the exit right here, before anything is locked.

exit_auction.open_exit(asset, amount, min_accept_bps)
2
Classify

The classifier assigns the tier and the band

The fresh score goes to risk_classifier, which returns the tier, the spread band in basis points and the routing policy. From this moment the exit's price ceiling and floor are fixed and public — nobody can bid outside them, including the protocol.

tier B → band [120, 300] bps → reserve: eligible
3
Price

Liquidity Nodes bid, or the reserve fills

If the exit qualifies for instant settlement and the reserve is above its floor, the vault fills it at the tier floor and the flow jumps to step 5. Otherwise the auction window opens for AUCTION_WINDOW and every Node whose appetite covers this tier can post a discount. Bids outside the band are rejected on entry, so the published band is enforced by the contract rather than by convention.

exit_auction.place_bid(exit_id, discount_bps) // must sit inside the band
4
Clear

Best bid wins — or the exit is queued

When the window closes, the lowest discount wins and is compared against the seller's min_accept_bps. If no bid clears that threshold, the exit is not force-filled at a bad price: it moves to fifo_queue with a public position and waits for capital. The seller keeps their tokens and their option to cancel.

no bid → fifo_queue.enqueue(exit_id) → emits exit.queued
5
Settle

One transaction moves everything

settlement_router.settle transfers the participation tokens to the winning Liquidity Node, the USDC to the seller net of the discount and protocol fee, and emits exit.settled with the full breakdown. Soroban's atomicity means there is no state where one leg happened and the other did not. A seller who wants fiat continues into an Anchor Platform withdrawal from the same USDC balance.

settle(exit_id) → tokens → LP | USDC → seller | fee → treasury
Chapter 04

Who provides the exit capital

A Liquidity Node is anyone willing to buy someone else's RWA position at a discount they set themselves. Permissionless to join, explicit about risk appetite, and never forced into a tier they did not opt into.

Phase 1

Deposit

Send USDC to lp_vault and become a Node. Capital sits idle-yield-bearing until it is used to fill an exit.

USDC on Stellar
Phase 2

Declare appetite

Choose which tiers you serve, the minimum discount you require, and your maximum exposure per asset. You are never matched outside this.

tiers · min_bps · max_exposure
Phase 3

Bid & win

Bid on open exits inside the tier band. Win and you receive the participation tokens at your discount — a yield-bearing position bought below score value.

exit.bid → exit.settled
Phase 4

Hold or re-exit

Keep collecting the asset's distributions, or open your own exit later and let the next Node price it. Nodes are not locked into what they buy.

distributions in USDC

Node parameters

served_tiers
The risk bands you are willing to underwrite. A Node that only serves A and B will never be shown a distressed exit, and its capital is not counted toward that tier's depth.
min_discount_bps
The floor return you require to part with capital. Bids you place are your own number — the protocol only enforces that it falls inside the tier's published band.
max_exposure
A per-asset ceiling on how much of a single RWA you can accumulate, so one asset going bad cannot take the whole position with it.
reserve_contribution
How much of your deposit may be used for instant hot-reserve fills. Opt in for higher utilisation, opt out to bid only where you have chosen to.

Withdrawing capital

Uncommitted capital
Anything not backing an open bid or an in-flight settlement can be withdrawn after the standard timelock. The delay exists so a Node cannot pull liquidity between bidding and settling.
Reserve-committed capital
Capital pledged to the hot reserve cannot be withdrawn below hot_reserve_floor while exits are settling against it. Instant settlement is only credible if the buffer cannot evaporate at the moment it is needed.
Chapter 04b

The Pooled Liquidity Node

Not everyone wants to manage tier appetites and bid on individual exits. The Pooled Node is a shared vault position that does it on behalf of its depositors.

How it works

One position, many depositors

The Pooled Node registers as a single Liquidity Node in lp_vault. Depositors contribute USDC and receive shares; the operator sets the tier appetite and bidding strategy for the whole position. Depositors get exposure to exit spreads without running a bidding operation.

Share-based

Proportional in, proportional out

Positions acquired through winning bids, and the distributions they generate, accrue to shareholders in proportion to their shares. Redemption is against the pool's share of vault capital plus the assets it currently holds.

💰

Passive participation

Deposit USDC, receive shares. No bidding, no per-asset parameters to maintain.

📈

Managed strategy

The operator adjusts which tiers to serve and at what discount as conditions change.

🌐

Multiple pools

Nothing stops several Pooled Nodes existing side by side with different risk mandates.

Chapter 05

When nobody bids

Most RWA venues have no answer for this case, which is why their liquidity claims break the first time an asset gets uncomfortable. DobDex makes the unfilled case an explicit, public protocol state.

30 min
auction window closes
→ no bid
#4
public FIFO position
You keep your tokens

A queued position is still yours and still earns whatever the underlying asset distributes while it waits.

Your position is visible

Queue depth and your place in it are readable on-chain by anyone. No private list, no discretionary reordering.

You can leave

Withdraw from the queue at any time and re-open later at a different threshold, or hold the asset to maturity.

💡
Why a visible queue matters more than a bigger promise
A venue that always claims instant liquidity is making a promise it cannot keep on a bad day, and the moment it breaks, everyone learns their exit was never real. A queue with a public position is a smaller promise that survives contact with reality — and it is the honest input a buyer needs when deciding what an asset is worth in the first place.
Chapter 06

Six Soroban contracts

The exit layer is deliberately small and separable. Each contract owns one decision, emits typed events, and is gated by multisig for every parameter that can move money.

score_readerIn build

Score Reader

Reads the TRUFA validation score published for an asset and exposes it on-chain with an explicit staleness window. A score older than STALENESS_SECS stops being usable for pricing instead of quietly going stale.

get_score(asset) → (score, age)
risk_classifierIn build

Risk Classifier

Maps a score to one of five tiers and to the spread band, reserve access and settlement path that tier is allowed. The tier table is a stored parameter, so policy can be tuned without redeploying the pricing path.

classify(score) → (tier, band, policy)
lp_vaultIn build

LP Liquidity Vault

Where Liquidity Nodes deposit USDC and declare which assets and tiers they will back. Enforces a hot-reserve floor so a single large exit cannot empty the vault, and routes idle capital to yield strategies.

deposit / withdraw / set_appetite
exit_auctionIn build

Exit Auction Engine

Opens a bounded AUCTION_WINDOW in which Liquidity Nodes bid to fill the exit. Best bid wins; the window is short enough to feel immediate and long enough to stop a single LP from dictating the price.

open_exit / place_bid / close
settlement_routerIn build

Settlement Router

Moves the participation token to the winning LP and USDC to the seller in a single atomic Soroban transaction, then emits the settlement event the indexer and the seller's receipt both read from.

settle(exit_id)
fifo_queueIn build

FIFO Queue Manager

When demand for the door exceeds available liquidity, exits enter a first-in-first-out queue instead of racing. Position is public, cancellation is allowed, and nobody can pay to jump the line.

enqueue / position_of / cancel

Typed events, indexed end to end

Every state change emits a typed Soroban event. An indexer subscribed to Soroban RPC reconstructs the entire history of exits, bids and vault movements without trusting our API.

exit.openedA holder opened an exit. Carries asset, amount, tier, spread band and the seller's minimum acceptable discount.
exit.bidA Liquidity Node posted a bid. Carries the Node, the discount in bps and the ledger it landed on.
exit.settledThe exit cleared. Carries the winning discount, USDC paid to the seller, the protocol fee and the counterparty.
exit.queuedNo acceptable bid. Carries the FIFO position assigned and the queue depth at that moment.
exit.cancelledThe seller withdrew before settlement, from the auction or from the queue.
vault.depositA Liquidity Node added USDC. Carries the Node, the amount and the resulting tier depth.
vault.withdrawCapital left the vault, after the timelock and the reserve-floor check.
vault.yieldYield accrued to vault capital while idle, and how it was attributed across Nodes.
⚡
Already proven on this stack
The Soroban event indexer is not speculative work — Dobprotocol already runs one against Stellar mainnet for its tokenization contracts, with 412 contract events indexed across 125 live pools. The DobDex build extends that same pipeline to the exit contracts rather than starting a new one.
Chapter 07

Safety rails

The failure modes that matter for an exit venue are stale data, drained reserves, front-run settlements and a governance key that can quietly change the rules. Each has a specific rail.

🕐

Staleness gate

No exit can be opened on an asset whose TRUFA score is older than the configured window. The contract refuses to quote rather than quoting on data nobody currently stands behind.

STALENESS_SECS: 24 hours
🛡

Hot reserve floor

Instant settlement stops automatically once the reserve reaches its floor. The buffer that makes fast exits credible cannot be drained to zero by a run of instant fills.

hot_reserve_floor (per tier)
⏱

Fixed auction window

The window is a protocol constant, not a per-exit choice. Nobody can shorten a window to squeeze out competing bids or extend one to wait for a friendlier counterparty.

AUCTION_WINDOW: 30 minutes
🔒

Band enforcement

Bids outside the tier's published spread band are rejected at entry. The band a seller was shown when they opened the exit is the band the contract enforces at settlement.

min_bps ≤ bid ≤ max_bps
👥

Seller floor price

Every exit carries the seller's min_accept_bps. A bid worse than that cannot settle — the exit queues instead. Sellers are never force-filled at a price they did not accept.

min_accept_bps in open_exit
🔑

Multisig-governed parameters

Tier boundaries, spread bands, the auction window, the staleness limit and the reserve floor are all on-chain values behind a multisig. Changing the rules is a public, signed, observable event.

multisig-gated config
⏳

Withdrawal timelock

Node withdrawals are time-delayed so liquidity cannot be pulled between placing a bid and settling it, and cannot be yanked out of the reserve the moment an exit needs it.

delayed withdraw request
📊

Per-asset exposure caps

Each Node sets a maximum exposure per asset, and each tier carries a protocol cap. Concentration risk is bounded on both sides rather than discovered afterwards.

max_exposure per node & tier
⏸

Emergency pause

Exit opening and settlement can be halted under multisig authority in a security incident. Pausing never strands assets: queued and unsettled positions remain the seller's property.

pause() / unpause()

Audit & monitoring plan

The build includes a written threat model covering oracle manipulation, reserve draining, bid front-running and governance-key compromise, plus an on-chain monitoring plan: alerting on reserve-floor breaches, abnormal queue growth, score-publication gaps and any parameter change. Contracts are open source and the event stream is public, so the monitoring we run is monitoring anyone can reproduce.

Built on Stellar
Ecosystem

What we build on

DobDex is deliberately not a self-contained island. Each piece of the exit path that Stellar already solves well is delegated to the ecosystem primitive that solves it.

Soroban

Build target

All six contracts are Rust on Soroban. Atomic multi-step settlement, typed events and predictable fees are the reason the exit path can be a single transaction instead of a choreography.

execution layer

DeFindex

Integration

Liquidity Node capital sitting in the vault waiting for exits should not sit dead. DeFindex strategies put idle reserve capital to work, so the opportunity cost of standing ready is lower — which is what makes deeper reserves economically rational.

idle capital yield

Blend v2

Integration

A Node that wants to bid on an exit without unwinding another position needs credit. Blend lets Nodes source short-term liquidity against collateral instead of keeping every dollar of bidding power idle in the vault.

node leverage

Anchor Platform

Integration

An exit that ends in USDC is only half an exit for an investor who needs local currency. The settlement router hands off to Anchor withdrawal flows so the last mile is fiat in a bank account, not another token to figure out.

fiat off-ramp

USDC on Stellar

Live

The single settlement asset for the entire protocol. Bids, fills, fees and queue positions are all denominated in it, so there is no second exchange-rate risk hidden inside the exit.

settlement asset

EVM reference build

Reference only

An earlier version of this design was built and tested against EVM. It de-risks the mechanism — the auction, tiering and queue logic have run before — but it is not the production target and is not where DobDex ships. Stellar is.
